Install

Your meetings, written down. No audio recording, no cloud.

Stenograf listens to a meeting on your own computer and writes down who said what, in German or English. When you stop, the transcript lands in your Documents folder, with notes if you asked for them. No audio is saved unless you ask for it.

why

Built for meetings you would not hand to a cloud service.

Stenograf runs entirely on your computer, keeps no audio recording, tells apart everyone who speaks, is as much at home in German as in English, works on Mac, Windows and Linux, and is free. Each of these exists elsewhere. Together they are hard to find.

private

Nothing is uploaded, and no audio is recorded.

Speech recognition and speaker separation run on your own computer. The audio is held in memory while the meeting runs and is gone when it ends, so there is no audio recording to store, share or delete. Keeping the audio is a switch you turn on yourself.

speakers

It knows who said what.

Your microphone and the sound of the call are captured separately, so your side and the remote side are never confused. It is built to tell apart people who share one microphone around a meeting-room table, and likewise several people on the call. Name a voice once and Stenograf recognises it in later meetings.

any call

It works with every call app, and with none.

Stenograf listens to two things: your microphone and whatever your computer is playing. That covers a call in Zoom, Teams, Meet or a browser, and equally a meeting where everyone sits around one table. No bot joins the call and the other side installs nothing. Laptop speakers are fine, because the echo of the remote voices is removed before anything is written down.

language

German and English, with your names and terms.

Each meeting is transcribed in German or in English. A list of the names and technical terms you use, kept in the settings, makes Stenograf spell them the way you do.

free

Free, with nothing held back.

Stenograf is open source. There is no account, no subscription and no limit on minutes, and no feature waits behind a paid plan. It does the same job on a Mac, on Windows and on Linux.

how it works

A meeting, from start to folder.

  1. Step 1

    Open Stenograf and choose Start meeting.

    You find it like any other app: Spotlight or the Dock on a Mac, the Start menu on Windows, the application menu on Linux. The start form works as it is. If you like, switch on notes or say how many people are in the room.

  2. Step 2

    Hold your meeting.

    Say at the start that the meeting is being written down, as you would if someone were taking minutes. Captions appear while people talk. You can close the window: Stenograf stays in the menu bar (the system tray on Windows and Linux), its icon turns red while it listens, and its menu can mute your microphone or the call.

  3. Step 3

    Choose Stop & finalize.

    The captions become the finished transcript: their words are kept, and every line gets its speaker. One card then asks what is left to decide: a title for the meeting, who the voices were, and whether to write notes. You can also discard the meeting there.

  4. Step 4

    Open the folder.

    Each meeting gets its own folder, named by date, inside Meetings in your Documents folder. There is no library to manage: read, move or delete the files as you would any others.

Already have a recording? Choose Transcribe a recording on the home screen and pick the audio or video file. You get the same transcript, speakers included.

what you get

A transcript and notes, as plain files.

  • The transcript names the speaker and the time for every line. It is ordinary Markdown, so it opens in any text editor and in tools such as Obsidian.
  • The notes sum up the meeting: what was discussed, the decisions, the action items with their owners, the statements worth keeping and the open questions. You can give them your own layout.
  • Other formats are a setting away: subtitle files (SRT, VTT), and one combined note per meeting written into a folder of your choice.
  • On a Mac the notes are written by a model running inside Stenograf. On Windows and Linux they need Ollama, a free program that runs such models on your computer.

install

Paste one line. After that it is an app like any other.

The installer sets up everything Stenograf needs, downloads the speech models once, and adds Stenograf to your apps. Pick your system:

macOS

Needs a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer) and macOS 14.4 or newer.

  1. Open Terminal: press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Return.
  2. Paste this line and press Return: 
    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/daniel-om-weber/stenograf/main/install.sh | sh
  3. Allow the permission requests when they appear. Stenograf needs your microphone and your computer's sound.
  4. Open Stenograf from Spotlight or the Dock.

Windows

Needs Windows 10 or 11 on an ordinary (x64) PC. Windows on ARM is not supported.

  1. Open PowerShell: press the Windows key, type PowerShell, press Enter.
  2. Paste this line and press Enter: 
    powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -c "irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/daniel-om-weber/stenograf/main/install.ps1 | iex"
  3. Windows never asks about the microphone by itself. If setup tells you it is off, switch it on under Settings › Privacy & security › Microphone.
  4. Open Stenograf from the Start menu.

Meeting notes on Windows need Ollama installed and running. The transcript works without it.

Linux

Needs a 64-bit Intel or AMD system (x86_64) with PipeWire or PulseAudio, and glibc 2.34 or newer.

  1. Open a terminal.
  2. Paste this line and press Enter: 
    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/daniel-om-weber/stenograf/main/install.sh | sh
  3. Open Stenograf from your application menu.

Meeting notes on Linux need Ollama installed and running. The transcript works without it.

The line downloads Stenograf's installer from its GitHub repository and runs it. The installer puts three things into your user account, without asking for an administrator password: uv (a small tool that manages Python programs), Stenograf itself, and the speech models. You can read it first: install.sh for macOS and Linux, install.ps1 for Windows. Later updates arrive inside the app.

questions

Before you install.

Does anything leave my computer?

Nothing about your meetings does. Stenograf goes online for two things: it downloads its models (during setup, and on a Mac the notes model the first time you ask for notes), and once a day it checks whether a newer version exists. That check tells the server only which version you have, and it can be switched off in the settings. The notes are written on your computer too, unless you connect a different notes program yourself.

What does it keep about voices?

For each speaker, a voiceprint: numbers that describe how the voice sounds, not audio. It sits in the meeting's folder. Voices you name are also kept in a voice library on your computer, so that later meetings recognise them. None of it is uploaded. Switch off Tell speakers apart and no voiceprints are kept.

What does it cost?

Nothing. Stenograf is open source under the MIT licence. The models it downloads come under their own licences, which the README lists.

Do I need headphones?

No. When remote voices come out of your speakers and reach your microphone, Stenograf removes that echo, so nobody is written down twice.

Which languages does it understand?

German and English, one language per meeting. Those are the two Stenograf is built and tested for. The speech model behind it also understands other European languages, so a meeting in French or Spanish is written down as well, but nobody has tested that on real meetings yet.

Can I keep the audio after all?

Yes. Switch on Keep the audio recording before you start, and the meeting folder also gets an audio file. Without that switch no audio is written.

How do updates work?

When a new version is out, a line appears at the foot of the app's home screen. Click Update: the new version is downloaded while you keep working, and the app restarts into it. This never happens during a meeting. If the new version does not start, the previous one is put back.

Something does not work. What now?

Open Check setup on the home screen. It tests the models, the permissions and your audio devices and says what is missing. If that does not help, report the problem on GitHub.

terminal

Everything is also a command.

The app is one way in. The same program runs in a terminal, in a script or over SSH, which also makes it a tool an AI agent can use.

  • Run it. steno start prints the captions line by line and writes the transcript when you stop it. steno transcribe does the same for a recording.
  • Read it. A meeting is a folder of Markdown and JSON files. A script or an agent reads them directly, with no database and no service in between.
  • Bring your own AI for the notes. The notes step can call any command-line program that takes a prompt and returns Markdown. It runs inside the meeting's folder, so an agent can look up the context your instructions point to. What you connect decides where the transcript goes: a program on your computer keeps it there, a cloud service does not.
  • All commands and settings are in the README.
# a meeting: captions appear here, transcript and notes when you stop
$ steno start --notes

# a recording you already have, printed to the terminal
$ steno transcribe interview.mov --print

# notes for the newest meeting
$ steno notes --last
